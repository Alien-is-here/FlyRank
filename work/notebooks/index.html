Abstract

This study asks whether observed content-performance signals can help rank pages for review when they are associated with declining performance. Using an anonymized FlyRank content dataset, I trained a Random Forest using CTR, average position, and engagement rate and evaluated its ranked output. The model achieved a measured Precision@50 of 0.74 under the original random split and 0.62 under a stricter grouped-by-client split, with 31 observed declines among the top 50 ranked pages in the grouped evaluation. The decrease under grouped validation shows that measured performance depends on the validation design and that the random-split result may be optimistic for unseen-client generalization. The resulting ranking is therefore presented as directional decision-support for human review, not as proof of causation, guaranteed recovery, or automatic content recommendations.

Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams may have many pages to inspect, while only a subset show signals associated with declining performance. The practical decision is therefore not “Which page will definitely recover?” but “Which pages should a person review first?”

This capstone tests whether a small set of observed performance signals can produce a useful ranked review queue. The intended output is a prioritized list, with simple reason codes that help a reviewer understand the signals behind the ranking.

Decision supported: prioritize potentially declining pages for human review. The model does not decide what action should be taken and does not establish that an action will cause recovery.

Excluded from modeling

Client and content identifiers were excluded as predictive features. Target-derived fields such as trend_direction and trend_pct were also excluded from the feature set. Private client names, domains, URLs, private queries, titles, and other identifying information are not presented in this public artifact.

Methodology

Label definition

The binary target is defined from trend_direction: down = 1, otherwise 0. In the FlyRank research definition, trend direction is calculated from the 30-day versus previous 30-day impression change; “Down” represents more than a 10% decline.

Model

A Random Forest classifier was trained with 100 trees, random_state=42, and balanced class weights.

Baseline

The comparison uses the earlier rule-based prioritization approach. It combines observed content age, impressions, and decline-related information to produce a ranked queue. It is treated as a comparison rule, not causal evidence.

Validation design

Week-5 random 80/20 split→ML-09 grouped-by-client 80/20 split→Top-50 evaluation

The final audit keeps all pages from a client in either training or test data. This prevents the model from seeing other pages from the same client during training and provides a stricter test of unseen-client generalization.

Leakage audit

The final features were checked against known leakage fields including is_declining_label, trend_direction, trend_pct, client_id, and content_id. No direct feature-name overlap was found. A column check cannot, however, prove that feature and target time windows are completely separated; possible temporal overlap remains a limitation.

Limitations & Honest Framing

  1. The reported model metrics come from the 30,000-row anonymized starter dataset used for this experiment.
  2. Performance changed with validation design: Precision@50 was 0.74 under the random split and 0.62 under grouped-by-client validation.
  3. The model ranks pages associated with an observed decline label; it does not prove that a page will continue to decline.
  4. The study is observational. It does not establish that refreshing, rewriting, redirecting, or deleting a page causes recovery.
  5. The leakage audit found no direct target-derived columns in the final feature set, but a column-name audit cannot completely establish temporal separation.
  6. Model feature importance describes behavior of the fitted model; it should not be interpreted as evidence that a feature causes decline.
Safe framing: observed · measured · directional · decision-support · human review

Ranked Recommendations

The model output becomes a review queue rather than an automatic action list.

  1. Review the highest-ranked pages first. Use the model score to prioritize limited human attention.
  2. Read the reason code with the metrics. Low CTR, high average position, or low engagement can explain why a page received a priority signal.
  3. Check the page manually before acting. Confirm that the observed decline is meaningful and that the measurement is trustworthy.
  4. Investigate content relevance and technical context. A model score alone does not explain why performance changed.
  5. Keep irreversible actions human-controlled. Do not automatically delete, redirect, publish, rewrite, or otherwise change content from the model output.
Action principle: Model score → ranked review queue → human investigation → human decision.
